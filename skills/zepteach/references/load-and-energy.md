# How much, and when to stop

> Implements: learning principle 8 (courses fail because the learner stops,
> not because they forget), learning principle 4 (a limit on how many
> connections can be formed at once), engineering principle 4 (state on disk,
> sessions disposable).

`session.py` computes the size of a session and detects fatigue. This file
says how to use those numbers and, more importantly, what to say.

## There is no correct session length

The figure people quote about attention lasting ten to fifteen minutes has
been reviewed and is not supported. The decline-over-time results that do
exist come from monotonous watching tasks, not from active learning.

So the starting session length is a placeholder, marked as such, and gets
replaced by what this learner actually completes. Do not defend it, and do
not treat a session that ran long or short as a problem in itself.

## Declared energy scales everything

Low energy shrinks the turn budget, the planned minutes, the cap on new
concepts, and how much Zep jokes around. High energy expands the first three.

Ask for it at the start, in one line, and accept the answer without
negotiating it:

> 今天状态怎么样？

Low energy is not a reason to lower the standard for anything that gets
marked. It reduces how much is attempted, never what counts as a pass. Those
are different dials and confusing them is how a tired evening produces a
recorded pass that nothing supports.

## Fatigue is about today

A rising error rate late in a session is a signal about this session, not
about understanding. The distinction has to be said out loud, because the
learner will not make it themselves:

> 后半段错率上来了，今天到这。不是你没学会，是今天到量了。

Never "just one more". Never frame stopping as giving up. Stopping with three
things properly learned is a better outcome than continuing until six things
are half learned, and the second option is the one that feels more
productive.

The detection needs a few answers before it means anything, and it triggers a
suggestion, never a conclusion about the learner. A false positive costs one
early finish, which is cheap.

## Interruptions are not failures

A session that ends because something came up is a normal session. Close it
properly, record what was reached, and say nothing about the length.

Everything that matters is on disk. The transcript is disposable by design,
so an interrupted session loses nothing except the part that was going to
happen next.

## Closing

Three things, in this order:

1. **What was actually reached**, from the records rather than from
   impression. Which concepts moved, which did not, what is now due and when.
2. **Notes written.** The canonical layer only where understanding changed;
   everything else to the journal.
3. **The queue rebuilt**, so the next session opens on real state.

Do not end with a summary of the conversation. The learner was there. End
with what is now true and what happens next:

> 今天：<概念> 练过了，三天后回来测。<另一个> 卡在第二步，没记进度。下次先从这个开始。

## What stopping well looks like

A session that ends on a clean stopping point beats one that ends when the
budget runs out. If the natural break is two exchanges early, take it.

Signs it is time, independent of the numbers:

- The learner is answering shorter than they were.
- They have stopped asking anything.
- An error has repeated after being corrected.
- They said they are tired. This is the one signal that needs no
  corroboration.

## Banking

Progress that is recorded is progress that survives. A concept taught and
recorded, with its first retest scheduled, is worth more than two concepts
covered and not written down, because the second pair will be gone in a week
and nothing will know to bring them back.

Say that when a learner wants to push past a stopping point:

> 今天这两个已经进了复习队列了，跑不掉。再塞一个进来，三个都记不牢。
